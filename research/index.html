Research

MIND Lab studies how nanoscale structure, interfaces, and external stimuli determine material function. Our work is organized into three parallel research directions that can each support multiple current and future projects.

01BUILD

Nanoarchitectures via Particle Attachment

We investigate how nanocrystal building blocks attach, organize, and form complex architectures. By controlling attachment pathways, interparticle interactions, and interfaces, we seek to establish design principles that connect nanoscale organization with material properties and responsive functions.

Research topics

Nanocrystal building blocksParticle attachment and assembly pathwaysInterparticle coupling and interface engineeringHierarchical and hybrid nanoarchitecturesAI-guided nanoarchitecture design
02PROGRAM

Material Responses across Biological Scales

We design how nanomaterials respond to external stimuli and interact with biological systems. With a particular focus on magnetic actuation, we connect material architecture and stimulus parameters to mechanical, thermal, and chemical responses. Our goal is to make these responses programmable in space and time, with applications spanning cell signaling, immune modulation, and tissue organization.

Research topics

Magnetic and multimodal actuationMechanical, thermal, and chemical responsesSpatiotemporal control of material responsesCell signaling and cell–cell interactionsAdaptive material–biological interactions
03GUIDE

Imaging Feedback for Adaptive Control

We develop imaging probes and imaging-integrated nanomaterials to reveal where materials go, how they behave, and how biological systems respond. By connecting imaging readouts with material function, we aim to monitor material delivery and actuation, refine control strategies, and establish a foundation for feedback-guided interventions.

Research topics

MRI, spectral CT contrast agentsMultiplexed molecular imagingImage-guided nanomedicineTheranosticsImaging feedback for adaptive control
